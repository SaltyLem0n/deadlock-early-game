01

THE PROBLEM

An early lead is only part of the story.

Deadlock combines hero abilities, combat, and a shared resource economy. An early advantage may change as a match develops. We want to understand when an outcome becomes more predictable, and whether the way souls are distributed across teammates adds useful information.

RQ / 01

How early can we tell?

How accurately can the final winning team be predicted at minutes 5, 10, and 15?

RQ / 02

Who holds the souls?

Do individual soul growth, team shares, and differences between teammates improve prediction beyond team totals, combat statistics, and hero composition?

RQ / 03

Which model holds up?

Do nonlinear models outperform a transparent baseline, and does performance change across match stages or game patches?

RELATED WORK

The comparison draws on interpretable logistic regression [3], tree ensembles [4], and gradient boosting [5]. These methods motivate the study; they do not establish performance on our Deadlock dataset. Chronological evaluation and leakage controls are central to the research design [7] [8].

03

THE METHOD

Same matches. Three checkpoints.

The proposal follows CRISP-DM: understand the problem, inspect the data, prepare features, model, evaluate, and publish reproducible outputs. The experiment compares team-only inputs against team inputs plus player soul features at each cutoff.

Cutoff explorerDESIGN / NOT A LIVE PREDICTOR

Minute 5 · early economy

Use only samples recorded at or before 05:00. Compare early team differences with each player's available soul history, growth, team share, and within-team spread.

All three cutoffs use this timing rule. Record actual timestamps and lag; compute growth over observed intervals. No match duration, final totals, or later objective events enter the predictors.

MODEL / 01 · PLANNED

Logistic Regression

An interpretable linear classifier to anchor the comparison. Fit preprocessing only on training data [3] [7].

MODEL / 02 · PLANNED

Random Forest

A tree ensemble to explore nonlinear relationships and feature interactions [4].

MODEL / 03 · CONDITIONAL

XGBoost

The proposal's optional third classifier, subject to time and team confirmation [5].

Course checkpoint: at least three algorithms are required by M3. Confirm XGBoost or select another third classifier before claiming this requirement is complete.

Beat a simple starting point.

Compare every model with a majority-class classifier [6] and a soul-lead rule. The rule predicts the team with more souls at the cutoff; tie handling must be specified before evaluation.

TEAM ONLY↔TEAM + PLAYER SOULS

Evaluate without looking ahead.

Train on older matches and test on newer matches. Keep every stage of a match in the same split; tune on training data only. Use identical held-out matches for fair model and feature-set comparisons [7] [8].

ACCURACYF1-SCOREROC-AUCCALIBRATION

Also inspect confusion matrices, patch robustness where feasible, and the added contribution of player soul features. Define repetitions or time windows before reporting means and standard deviations; a single run is not a variability estimate.
